6.1.1.1. gemini_model.absorption_chiller.absorption_chiller

Single-effect LiBr/H2O absorption chiller driven by geothermal hot water (GM-04).

Source: RC-05 (data/knowledge/reference_cards/RC-05_absorption_chiller.md) and the copy in REFERENCE.md; Geothermal Direct Use Engineering & Design Guidebook (Geo-Heat Center 1989) Ch14 p.261-270; spec §3.3 and §7.2 GM-04.

Two methods (parameter method):

  • "ghc1989" – Method A, the Guidebook correlation (ghc1989). Capacity F Q_nom with F = F_T(T_hw) F_c(T_chw, T_cw), COP(T_hw). Valid for 40-50 °F chilled and 75-90 °F cooling water; outside, it is a conservative lower bound (RC-05): with ghc1989_conservative_clamp (default) chilled water above 50 °F is evaluated at 50 °F and cooling water below 75 °F at 75 °F (F rises with T_chw and falls with T_cw, so this never overstates capacity); other excursions are extrapolated. Part load: COP(T_hw).

  • "characteristic" (default) – Method B, the ΔΔt characteristic equation (characteristic), needed for our 20 °C chilled water. Coefficients are calibrated to Method A at its rating points by characteristic.calibrate_to_ghc1989() unless characteristic_coefficients gives datasheet values. Part load is a reduction of ΔΔt (drive-temperature control): ΔΔt = ΔΔt_min + Q_E/s_E and Q_G = s_G (ΔΔt - ΔΔt_min,G), so the COP falls slightly at part load. Results at 20 °C chilled water are extrapolated from the 6.7 °C calibration and are uncertain (RC-05 screening [D]: 85 °C drive at 20 °C chilled ≈ capacity fraction 0.6-0.7, COP 0.63-0.70).

Drive-side consistency (COOL-ABS-DRV): with a given drive flow, Method B needs a drive capacity rate of at least s_G/2 (output minimum_drive_flow_rate_m3s), otherwise the drive outlet would fall below the machine’s minimum generator temperature and the machine is treated as off; Method A limits the drive heat so that the outlet stays at or above its zero-capacity temperature (71.4 °C). Part load (COOL-ABS-PL): Method B’s COP falls at low load (≈ 0.47 at 10 % load at 85 °C drive) and the drive heat drops to 0 only when the machine stops.

External circuits: each circuit either has a given flow (its outlet temperature follows from the energy balance and, for Method B, its mean temperature is solved together with the duty – the equations are linear, so the solution is closed-form) or no flow (fixed temperature change: generator generator_delta_t_k 7.2 K [L p.267], cooling water cooling_water_range_k 5 K [A RC-07]; the required flow is reported). The chilled side is set either by cooling_load_w (supply held at the set point, flow = Q_E / (ρ c_p ΔT); unmet load is reported, not passed through) or by chilled_water_flow_rate_m3s (load = ρ c_p V (T_return - T_supply,set)). Each circuit has its own density and heat capacity (defaults: GEMINI water PVT, 1000 kg/m³, 4217.4 J/(kg K)); set the drive side to the brine values (1030, 4100) when brine flows through titanium generator tubes.

Outputs: cooling_power_w Q_E, drive_heat_w Q_G = Q_E/COP, heat_rejected_w = Q_E + Q_G + P_aux (exact), power_el = P_aux = aux_power_fraction Q_E (0.077 kW/ton ≈ 2.2 %: extra tower fan + cooling-water pump vs an electric chiller, Guidebook p.269), power_th = Q_E (useful cooling), emission 0, circuit outlet temperatures and flows, validity_warnings. include_heat_rejection_auxiliaries (default True) switches P_aux on; set it to False when a heat_rejection component is connected, which counts the tower fan and pumps itself (RC-05/RC-06 no-double-counting rule; P_aux = 0 and Q_reject = Q_E + Q_G).

Materials (RC-05, p.266-267): Cu / 90-10 CuNi generator tubes are incompatible with geothermal brine -> titanium generator tubes (+10-15 % machine cost) or an isolation HEX with a clean loop (then the drive inlet is the clean-loop temperature).

OIT case (Guidebook p.263) – documented comparison, not a pass/fail test: the book selected a nominal 312 ton machine for a 150 ton duty at 190 °F (F ≈ 0.48), whereas Method A at 44/85 °F gives F = 0.382 (≈ 392 ton) and at 48 °F chilled water F = 0.416; the case conditions are not fully stated in the extracted text. Method A is conservative.
